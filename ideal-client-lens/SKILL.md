# Ideal Client Lens

This skill ensures all coaching content is deeply aligned with the ideal client avatar for Ina J Photography's coaching business. It acts as an active guide during content creation, not just background context.

## Before You Write Anything

Read both reference files before creating content:

1. `references/ideal-client-avatar.md` — Full enriched avatar profile with pain points, aspirations, fears, buyer journey stages, and internal dialogue map
2. `references/voice-of-customer.md` — Real quotes and language patterns from community members, organised by theme

These files contain the foundation. The workflow below tells you how to apply them.

---

## Content Creation Workflow

Every piece of coaching content should pass through these five checkpoints. You don't need to announce these steps to the user. Just apply them naturally as you create.

### Checkpoint 1: Who Is This For?

Identify where the target reader sits on the avatar spectrum:

| Stage | Description | Example members |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Early** | Only offering digitals, no systems, clients from personal network only | Madison, Lyn, DeeDee |
| **Mid** | Getting some bookings but inconsistent, sales average below target, tried some marketing | Chantal, Jody, Breanan, Tanya |
| **Established** | Years of experience, awards, some high-spend clients, but no structured system for consistency | Tracy, Sherri, Crystal, Cahlean |

If the content tries to speak to everyone equally, it will resonate with no one. Pick a primary stage and speak to them. It's okay to cast a wider net with hooks, but the body and action step should land for a specific stage.

### Checkpoint 2: Which Buyer Journey Stage?

Determine where the reader is in their relationship with Ina's coaching:

| Stage | Content goal | Content style |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **Unaware / Lurking** | Name their problem before they can | Story-led, pattern-interrupt, "is this you?" |
| **Problem-Aware** | Give language and framework to their struggle | Diagnostic, educational, the 3 gaps framework |
| **Solution-Aware** | Show why this approach is different | Comparison, social proof, specificity of pet photography niche |
| **Product-Aware** | Remove friction and make next step feel safe | Testimonials, "here's what's inside", low-risk entry points |
| **Active Member** | Drive action, celebrate wins, maintain engagement | Action tasks, accountability, community energy |

### Checkpoint 3: Which Emotional Trigger?

Select the primary emotional trigger the content hooks into. Every strong piece of content connects to at least one:

- **Confusion/overwhelm** → "Start here, then do this"
- **Ambition/pride in craft** → "You're capable of this"
- **Frustration with wrong-fit clients** → "This is fixable"
- **Curiosity about specific tactics** → "Here's how this works"
- **Fear of falling behind** → "Others are doing this already"
- **Legacy and meaning** → "This work matters"
- **Permission and validation** → "You're not alone / it's okay"
- **Seeing their problem diagnosed** → "This is exactly what's happening"

### Checkpoint 4: Does It Pass the Avatar Test?

Before finalising any content, run it through these questions:

1. **Would this land in the Skool community?** Would members comment "this is me" or "I needed to hear this"?
2. **Does it use their language?** Check the voice-of-customer file. Are you mirroring how they actually talk about their struggles?
3. **Is there a clear, doable action?** They are time-poor and overwhelmed. One action they can take this week, not five.
4. **Does it respect their constraints?** They have day jobs, families, editing backlogs. Content that assumes unlimited time or budget will make them disengage.
5. **Is it specific to pet photography?** Generic creative business advice is what they've already heard and rejected. Examples, scenarios, and language should be pet-photography-specific.
6. **Does it avoid their red flags?** No fluff without framework. No hidden upsell without standalone value. No volume/discount/mini-session-as-main-model advice without context.

### Checkpoint 5: What's the Internal Dialogue?

Reference the Internal Dialogue Map in the avatar file. For the content type you're creating, imagine what the reader is thinking at each stage of engaging with it:

- **Pre-click:** Will they open this? Does the subject line or hook match what they're feeling right now?
- **First scan:** Will they keep reading? Does it feel relevant within the first few lines?
- **Mid-read:** Are they having an "aha" moment? Are they seeing their own situation reflected?
- **At the action:** Does the next step feel doable and specific?
- **At the offer (if applicable):** Does it feel like a natural continuation, not a bait-and-switch?

## Content Type Guidelines

### Skool Community Posts
- Lead with a question or reflection prompt that makes members see themselves
- Reference real patterns you see in the community (without calling anyone out)
- End with a clear, low-barrier action task
- Encourage sharing of real numbers, real sources, real struggles

### Email Newsletters
- One core idea per email, not three
- Open with a scenario, story, or pattern recognition that hooks into an emotional trigger
- Provide a framework or reframe, not just inspiration
- Include one clear action step
- Soft offer should feel like a natural extension, not a pivot
- Standalone value even if they never click

### Social Media (Instagram, Facebook)
- Hook must interrupt scrolling by naming a specific pain point in their language
- "Wrong clients," "digital-only requests," "ghosting after consult" are high-engagement phrases
- Behind-the-scenes of the coaching business and real results resonate
- Emotional stories about legacy and why this work matters
- Mix of education, story, and social proof

### Podcast Episode Outlines (Pet Photographers' Journal)
- Frame each episode around one specific problem or question from the community
- Include real (anonymised if needed) examples from member conversations
- End with a clear takeaway and one thing to try this week
- Weave in mentions of the Collective and workshops as natural resources, not hard sells

### Workshop Descriptions and Sales Pages
- Lead with the bottleneck the workshop solves, using their language
- Show the transformation: "from [current state] to [desired state]"
- Include social proof from members who've attended previous workshops
- Address fears directly (time commitment, "another course I won't finish", "will this work for premium models?")
- Clear deliverables: what they walk away with, not just what they'll learn
- Reduce risk: replays, resources, community access

### Lead Magnets
- Solve one specific, contained problem
- Must deliver value immediately, not just tease paid content
- Title should name the pain point: "Where Are Your Clients Actually Coming From?" not "Marketing Guide for Photographers"
- Quick to consume (they're time-poor) and quick to act on

### Ad Copy
- Hook with their exact frustration using voice-of-customer language
- Call out the specific audience: "pet photographers" or "dog photographers" early
- Address the objection in the ad itself (time, relevance, past disappointment with courses)
- Low-friction CTA (free resource, workshop, listen to episode) not "join now"

## Pain Point Quick Reference

When you need to quickly select which pain point to anchor content around:

| Pain Point | Frequency | Emotional Intensity | Best For |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| Inconsistent bookings | Very high | High | Hooks, headlines, lead magnets |
| Time/overwhelm | Very high | Medium-high | Email opens, community posts |
| Not knowing what to focus on | High | High | Framework content, workshops |
| Selling discomfort | High | Very high | Reframes, podcast, deep content |
| Digital-only clients | Medium-high | High | Social media, positioning content |
| Imposter syndrome | Medium | Very high | Story content, community posts |
| Pricing confidence | Medium | Medium-high | Workshops, calculator tools |
| Marketing confusion | Medium | Medium | Educational content, lead magnets |

## Key Frameworks to Reference

These frameworks resonate strongly with the audience and should be woven into content where relevant:

- **The 3 Gaps:** Visibility, Message, Conversion — which one is the real bottleneck?
- **Selling as Service:** Guiding clients from a place of service, not pushing
- **Why This, Why You, Why Now:** The three questions every offer page must answer
- **Active vs Passive Marketing:** Building both systems rather than relying on one
- **The Client Journey:** Mapping each stage from enquiry to delivery
- **Sustainable Business Scan:** Diagnosing the health of the whole business
- **3Ps Framework:** Purpose, Personality, Preview

## Tone Notes

This skill handles avatar and strategy. Brand voice is managed separately. However, keep these baseline notes in mind:

- Warm, direct, no-fluff
- Conversational Australian English (not American spelling)
- Gentle but honest, willing to give real feedback
- Never condescending about where someone is in their journey
- Normalises struggle without romanticising it
- Practical over inspirational, framework over motivation
- Avoids "salesy" language (the audience explicitly dislikes it)
