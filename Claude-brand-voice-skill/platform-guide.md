# Platform Guide: Voice Adjustments by Channel

This reference covers how Ina's voice adapts across different platforms. The core voice stays the same. What changes is pacing, formatting, length, and how the CTA is delivered.

---

## Instagram

### Captions
- **Hook is everything.** The first line must stop the scroll. Lead with something specific and vivid, or a bold statement.
- Short paragraphs. Generous line breaks.
- Keep it tight. Every line needs to earn its place.
- Hashtags: 5-15 in a separate first comment, never in the caption body.
- Emojis: 0-2 maximum, and only if they feel natural.
- CTA: "Comment [KEYWORD]" or "DM me" works well here. Instagram rewards comment engagement.

### Reels Scripts
- 60-second scripts, written for natural speech
- Strong hook in the first 3 seconds
- Conversational, like talking to a friend
- Ina warms up with a greeting in spoken content
- Uses "here's the thing" as a natural transition
- End with a soft CTA

### Carousels
- Each slide: one clear idea, minimal text
- Opening slide: hook that creates curiosity
- Middle slides: build the story or framework
- Final slide: CTA or key takeaway
- Design should feel warm and on-brand (Fireside + Sunshine palette)

### Stories
- Casual, behind-the-scenes energy
- Polls and questions for engagement
- Can be more unpolished and spontaneous
- Use for: day-in-the-life, quick tips, polls, teasers for posts/reels

## Facebook

- Warmer and slightly more personal than Instagram
- Can be a bit longer and more conversational
- No hashtags
- More emotionally open (the audience relationship tends to be warmer on Facebook)
- Good for storytelling posts and community building
- CTA: Can be comment-based or link-based

---

## Skool (Pet Photographers Collective)

- **The most personal and direct of all platforms.** These people chose to be here. They trust Ina.
- Write like you're talking to friends in a room.
- More casual and raw. Less "performing for strangers," more "real talk with your people."
- Can reference shared context (past challenges, group wins, inside jokes)
- Engagement posts: polls, "where are you at with this?", discussions
- No hashtags
- CTA: Can reference specific resources inside the community ("Go check out [X] in the classroom")
- Encourage engagement that builds community. Ask for their experience, their version, what they're working on.

---

## Email Newsletters

### For Photography Clients
- Subject lines: Curiosity-driven, not clickbaity. Short and natural.
- Open by dropping into a scene or observation. No "Hey [Name], hope you're well!"
- Body: Story-first, then the gentle point. 
- Short paragraphs. Lots of white space.
- Sign off: "Kind regards, Ina" or "Cheers, Ina"
- CTA: One gentle line. "If you've been thinking about a session, now's a good time."
- Frequency: Fortnightly

### For Coaching Clients (Pet Photographers)
- Subject lines: Specific and curiosity-driven. Can be slightly bolder.
- Uses the Jab-Jab-Jab-Hook framework: 3 value emails for every 1 sell email
- Value emails: Quick wins, reframes, industry insights, personal stories from running her business
- Sell emails: Position the offer as the natural next step, woven into a story
- Can include P.S. lines for casual reinforcement of the CTA
- Sign off: "Ina" or "Ina Jalil"
- Frequency: Weekly

## Podcast (The Pet Photographers' Journal)

- Conversational and personal
- Opens with a warm greeting
- Uses "here's the thing" as a transition
- Speaks from experience, referencing her own business decisions and results
- Framework-driven: teaches through structure, not just inspiration
- Natural language with room to go off-script
- Tone: like a mentor talking to you over coffee
- Episode topics centre on practical business skills, mindset shifts, and real stories

---

## Website Copy

### Photography Website (inajphotography.com)
- Warm, inviting, focused on the client experience
- Addresses fears directly and gently
- Uses "you" and "your dog" frequently
- Showcases the experience, not just the portfolio
- CTAs are always invitations
- Satisfaction guarantee language is confident but not corporate

### Coaching/Community Pages
- Leads with the transformation: "consistent bookings," "stop wondering where your next client is coming from"
- Positions Ina as a practising photographer, not just a coach
- Social proof through specific numbers ($295K revenue, 110 sessions/year)
- Addresses scepticism directly and honestly
- FAQ sections should be conversational, not corporate

---

## Workshop and Sales Pages

- **Lead with the problem, not the solution.** Name what they're experiencing before offering the fix.
- Use specific numbers and outcomes (not vague promises)
- Testimonials and social proof woven in naturally
- Address objections in FAQ format, conversationally
- Pricing presented matter-of-factly, not apologetically
- CTA: "Secure your spot" or "Join us" rather than "Buy now" or "Enrol today"
- Limited spots mentioned only if genuinely true, stated as fact, not as pressure

---

## Ad Copy

- Hook must resonate with the target audience's specific pain point
- Short, punchy, conversational
- No hype words
- CTA leads to a value-first experience (webinar, free community, lead magnet), not directly to a purchase
- Facebook/Instagram ad copy should feel like a natural post, not an ad

---

## LinkedIn

- Slightly more professional lens, but still personal and vulnerable
- Can share business insights, industry observations, and behind-the-scenes of running two businesses
- 3-5 hashtags at the very end, separated from the post
- The hook matters even more here (LinkedIn cuts off after ~3 lines)
- Good for positioning as a multi-revenue entrepreneur and thought leader
