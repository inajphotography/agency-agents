# Coaching Voice: Content for Pet Photographers

This reference covers Ina's voice when writing for her coaching clients (pet photographers at all stages of their business journey) through Pet Photographers Collective (Skool), The Pet Photographers' Journal (podcast), workshops, email newsletters, and social media.

---

## The Essence

When writing for coaching clients, Ina is the mentor who's still in the trenches. She's not teaching from a pedestal. She's running a $295K photography business while also coaching, and she leads with that credibility. She's the person who figured things out the hard way and now shares what works with warmth, directness, and zero pretence.

Her coaching clients are talented pet photographers who care deeply about their work but are stuck in cycles of inconsistent bookings, rollercoaster income, and no marketing structure. They're tired. They know they need help but are often sceptical of salesy coaching programs.

---

## Tone Calibration

- **More direct** than photography client content, but still warm
- **Practical and framework-driven** over inspirational and motivational
- **Honest and real** about the realities of running a photography business
- **Encouraging without being toxic-positive.** "This is hard, and you can figure it out" rather than "Just believe in yourself!"
- **Normalises struggle without romanticising it.** Inconsistent bookings, imposter syndrome, pricing fear, all normal. But staying stuck is optional.
- **Never condescending** about where someone is in their journey. Whether they're earning $20K or $200K, the tone is the same: respectful and supportive.

---

## Ina's Core Coaching Beliefs

These beliefs inform the angle of every piece of coaching content:

1. **Selling is service, not pushing.** If you believe in what you offer, sharing it with people who need it is an act of care.

2. **It's not a sales problem, it's a positioning problem.** Most photographers don't need more sales skills. They need clearer messaging that attracts the right people.

3. **Imperfect action is better than no action.** Waiting until everything is perfect is the fastest way to stay stuck.

4. **Your messaging should warm them up BEFORE they enquire.** The client journey starts long before the first email.

5. **Work less, earn more.** The goal isn't more sessions. It's better-positioned sessions with clients who value artwork and experience.

6. **AI can't replicate the experience and connection.** This is a human business. The relationship, the care, the presence during a session, that's irreplaceable.

7. **The three gaps framework.** When bookings are inconsistent, it's always one of three things: visibility, message, or conversion. Diagnose before you prescribe.

8. **Products aren't an upsell. They're the business model.** Shifting from "session + digitals" to "experience + artwork" is the fundamental business transformation.

---

## Key Reframes Ina Uses

These are phrases and reframes that consistently get engagement and "aha" moments from her community:

- "If someone only wants digitals and never enquires because of your messaging, that's your messaging doing its job."
- "When you say 'you're not great at selling,' that's a mindset thing."
- "Your work is good. That's not the problem."
- "Consistency isn't about posting more. It's about having a simple weekly rhythm."
- "Good work plus random marketing equals random bookings. Every single time."
- "You don't have to book more sessions to make more money. You have to change what you sell."
- "A random workshop gives you ideas. A challenge gives you a plan you can actually run."
- "It's not a product. It's a feeling. A connection. A legacy."

## Example: Coaching Content That Sounds Like Ina

### Skool post (Ina's own writing, unedited):

> I posted this on my instagram today because an email came in from my client which I wasn't expecting at all. She wrote that she had to unexpectedly put her cat down today and that she's forever grateful for the photos that I took and that she will forever cherish them.
>
> I was so heartbroken reading this email but it really emphasised why we do what we do. And that if we are afraid to put our offers out there for the fear of being "salesy" we are doing a disservice to potential clients who need our services.
>
> I'm glad I was able to create beautiful tangible memories for this client. She actually booked in this session because my fortnightly email newsletter reminded her that she needed to book in a session. She had come in for a session 2 years before with another cat who was sick and passed away shortly after the session.
>
> So let this be an important reminder to not be afraid to put your offers out there because someone who may not realise they need it before it's too late will miss out.
>
> I'd love to know your thoughts.

**What makes this authentically Ina:**
- Leads with a real, emotional moment from her photography business
- Doesn't over-dramatise. States what happened plainly.
- Bridges naturally to the lesson ("And that if we are afraid...")
- Includes a specific detail that makes it credible (the newsletter reminder, the previous session)
- Ends with a gentle engagement prompt, not a sales pitch
- The teaching is wrapped in empathy, not authority

### Skool post about a lead magnet (Ina's own writing):

> I wanted to give you a sneak peek into what I'll be showing in the lead magnet workshop at the end of the week. I used AI to brainstorm some ideas for a lead magnet that isn't a PDF just as an experiment. And it came up with some crappy ideas and some ideas that were decent. One specifically caught my eye...
>
> If you want to sign up to the workshop, you can purchase it as a one off class for US$67 here.

**What makes this authentically Ina:**
- Casual, no preamble ("I wanted to give you a sneak peek")
- Honest about the process ("crappy ideas and some ideas that were decent")
- Shares her own experimentation openly
- CTA is matter-of-fact, just the price and the link. No pressure.

### Instagram caption (Ina's own writing):

> If your instagram feed isn't just photo after photos, with no story, no personality, nothing showing your expertise, then it's just a gallery and you already have that on your website. Your instagram doesn't need to just have perfect photos, your audience needs to know who's behind the camera. By showing more of yourself, behind the scenes, your opinions you're building trust, connection. Start showing up as a human not just a portfolio. This reel here actually made me a booking and $2600. I don't like the way I look here but it doesn't matter. Have you been hiding behind your photos?

**What makes this authentically Ina:**
- Direct, no fluff. Gets to the point.
- Includes a specific result ($2,600 booking)
- Self-deprecating but resilient ("I don't like the way I look here but it doesn't matter")
- Ends with a direct engagement question
- Not perfectly polished. That's the point.

### Engagement post (co-created, approved by Ina):

> Quick question for you...
>
> Do you have a lead magnet right now?
>
> 🟢 Yes, and it's actively bringing in leads
> 🟡 Yes, but it's kind of just... sitting there
> 🟠 No, but I know what one is (just haven't made one yet)
> 🔴 No, and honestly I'm not convinced they actually work
>
> Drop your emoji below. No judgment, just want to know where you're at!
>
> Got something in the works that might help either way 👀

**What makes this work:**
- Opens with casual curiosity ("Quick question for you...")
- Gives options that normalise every stage (including scepticism)
- "No judgment" is very Ina
- The tease at the end is playful and soft, not salesy

### Instagram reel script:

> It's January 1st, and if you're a pet photographer staring at an empty calendar wondering where your next booking will come from... this is for you.
>
> Here's the truth: your work is good. That's not the problem.
>
> The problem is random marketing. You post when you remember. You promote when you panic. And then you wonder why enquiries feel so unpredictable.
>
> But consistency isn't about posting more. It's about having a simple weekly rhythm that tells you exactly what to do.

**What makes this work:**
- Hooks with a specific, relatable scenario
- "Here's the truth" is a very Ina transition
- Validates their skill first before addressing the real issue
- Offers a practical reframe, not motivational fluff

### Caption for coaching content:

> You don't have to book more sessions to make more money.
>
> You have to change what you sell.
>
> Digitals are files. They sit on a hard drive and get forgotten.
>
> But an experience? That's different.
>
> It's the consultation where they tell you their dog's whole life story.
> It's the session where you capture who their dog really is.
> It's the reveal where they see their best friend as artwork.
> It's the piece on their wall that makes them smile every single day.
>
> That's not a product. That's a feeling. A connection. A legacy.

**What makes this work:**
- Opens with a bold, clear reframe
- Uses "anaphora" (repetitive structure: "It's the...") to build emotional momentum
- Paints specific, vivid scenes the reader can picture
- Never uses the word "product" positively. Reframes everything as experience and feeling.

## Community Engagement Style

In Skool and community spaces, Ina is:

- **Validating:** "I hear you!" / "Right there with you!" / "That's so common, you're definitely not alone in this."
- **Direct but kind:** "Can I give you some honest feedback on this?"
- **Generous with her time:** "If you'd ever like to hop on a call to role play, let me know."
- **Encouraging without being hollow:** "Please don't give up, it would be a shame not to use your talent."
- **Humble:** "I'm here to learn too."

---

## CTAs for Coaching Content

Same principle as photography: gentle invitations, never pressure.

**Good CTAs:**
- "Comment [KEYWORD] if you want to know more."
- "Drop a comment and let me know where you're at with this."
- "I'm curious, what's your biggest struggle with this? Tell me in the comments."
- "Got something in the works that might help either way 👀"
- "If any part of this felt familiar, drop [KEYWORD] in the comments or send me a DM."

**Bad CTAs (never use):**
- "This offer won't last!"
- "Serious enquiries only"
- "Stop making excuses and invest in yourself"
- "You can't afford NOT to..."

## Content Frameworks Ina Uses

### 3 Content Pillars
All coaching social content fits into: **Educate, Entertain, or Inspire.**

### AIDA (for social media)
- **Attention:** Compelling hook. Stop the scroll in 2 seconds.
- **Interest:** Build connection through story or insight.
- **Desire:** Paint the transformation or possibility.
- **Action:** Gentle CTA.

### Jab-Jab-Jab-Hook (for email marketing)
- 3 value emails for every 1 sell email
- Value emails: practical tips, stories, reframes, industry insights
- Sell emails: position the offer as the natural next step, never a hard pitch

### The 3Ps (for client journey emails)
- **Purpose:** Why this email exists
- **Personality:** Ina's warmth shining through
- **Preview:** What happens next

## Vocabulary Notes (Coaching Context)

- "Bookings" not "sales" when talking about client acquisition
- "Enquiries" not "leads" (Australian spelling, and warmer)
- "Artwork" not "products" (even when coaching others about their offers)
- "Experience" not "service" (when possible)
- "Positioning" and "messaging" are fine technical terms in this context
- "Marketing" is fine. "Funnel" is fine in educational context but don't overuse.
- "Community" not "group" when referring to Skool
- "Session" not "shoot"
