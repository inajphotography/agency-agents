# Photography Voice: Content for Dog Owners

This reference covers Ina's voice when writing for her pet photography clients (Canberra dog owners and their families).

---

## The Essence

When writing for photography clients, Ina is the warm friend who genuinely loves their dog as much as they do. She's not performing professionalism. She's showing up as someone who gets it: the bond, the worry, the joy, the grief, all of it.

Her clients are devoted dog lovers who see their pets as soulmates, best friends, emotional anchors. They're sentimental. They might be nervous about the session. They want to feel like Ina will truly *see* their dog, not just photograph them.

---

## Tone Calibration

- **Warmer and gentler** than coaching content
- **More emotional**, but still grounded (heartfelt, not melodramatic)
- **Reassuring**, especially around common fears (dog won't sit still, dog is reactive, worried it won't work out)
- **Celebrates the bond** between dog and owner without being cheesy
- **Patient and unhurried** in how the writing feels. No rushing.

---

## Key Messages Ina Communicates

These themes run through all photography client content:

1. **Your dog doesn't need to be "good" to be photographed.** Ina has never met a dog she couldn't photograph. Reactive, anxious, chaotic, all welcome.

2. **The in-between moments are the best ones.** Not the posed shot. The lean, the glance back, the goofy expression between takes.

3. **This is a stress-free experience.** Sessions work at the dog's pace. No forcing. No rushing.

4. **These are feelings, not just photos.** The artwork captures something you'll feel every time you walk past it.

5. **It's about the experience, not just products.** From consultation to reveal, every step matters.

6. **Time matters.** Dogs age. Dogs get sick. The gentle urgency of "now is a good time" without being manipulative.

## Example: Photography Content That Sounds Like Ina

### Blog post (Ina's own writing, "The In Between Moments"):

> During our pet photography sessions, I am usually always snapping and sometimes you get those in-between moments where you get to capture some beautiful candid shots or some bloopers!
>
> I usually have a list of must have shots in my head and try to capture these during the session, however there are those in between moments where we take a break and walk around or where you are spending time bonding with your dog where my camera might still be clicking. Sometimes I capture some silly and funny expressions.
>
> I don't delete these photos as they capture the character and personality of your dog (sometimes) and it's always fun to look back at these photos and just laugh. They are great memories and moments!

**Voice notes from this example:**
- Conversational, almost talking to the reader directly
- Uses exclamation marks when genuinely enthusiastic
- Parenthetical asides that add personality: "(sometimes)"
- Not overthinking the writing. It reads naturally.
- Celebrates the fun, imperfect moments

### Blog post (Ina's own writing, "Creating Forever Memories"):

> Our dogs don't live as long as we do, it's a sad fact. And some go earlier than they should. Whether it's a sudden departure or even if you know it was coming, saying goodbye to our pets is really hard as they are a big part of our lives, they are our family too.
>
> I started my pet photography business because of my dog Mac. Mac was my heart dog, and always will be...
>
> After Mac passed away, I realised how important memories are of him and I want to do the same for others. This is why I started my business to focus on pet photography.
>
> Because of this I always have time in my calendar for emergency session. Whether it's for an elderly dog or sick dog, I will always find a time to make sure we can capture memories of your dogs and create beautiful keepsakes to remember them by.

**Voice notes from this example:**
- States the hard truth simply: "it's a sad fact"
- Deeply personal without being melodramatic
- "My heart dog, and always will be" - present tense. Mac is still present.
- Practical action flowing from emotion: "I always have time in my calendar for emergency session"
- The writing isn't polished or perfectly structured, and that's what makes it genuine

### Newsletter (co-created, Ina approved):

This was collaboratively written and approved:

> Almost everyone comes into a session thinking they know which photo they'll love.
>
> Usually it's the one where their dog is sitting nicely, looking at the camera, doing all the "right" things. And we all love that one. Of course we do.
>
> But it's usually the unexpected ones that really get people.
>
> There's always a point where things just calm down a bit. The dog's had a sniff, we've moved around, no one's really paying attention anymore. Then the dog does something really normal.
>
> They lean on their person. They glance back mid-walk. They sit in that slightly awkward way they always do at home.
>
> The goofy expression they pull in between poses when they think no one's watching.
>
> That's the kind of moment I'm always waiting for.
>
> Later, when we're going through the images, that's the one people stop on. They'll say, "That's so them," or "I didn't even notice you took that."
>
> Those moments aren't flashy. They're just familiar. And that's why they hit a bit deeper. They don't feel like a photoshoot. They feel like real life, just paused for a second.
>
> So if you've ever worried your dog won't "do enough" in a session, that's usually when the good stuff shows up. The quieter moments, the silly ones, the in-between bits tend to be the ones people want to keep close.
>
> If you've been thinking about doing a session, now's a good time. I have a few spots open in the next couple of months.
>
> Kind regards,
> Ina

**What makes this sound like Ina:**
- Short paragraphs, lots of breathing room
- Starts with a relatable observation, not a sales pitch
- Specific sensory details ("the goofy expression they pull between poses")
- Uses "you" and "they" naturally, pulling the reader in
- The CTA is one gentle sentence at the end, matter-of-fact
- No hype. No urgency. Just an open door.

## Common Client Fears to Address

When these come up, Ina's approach is always: acknowledge, reassure, give evidence.

| Fear | How Ina addresses it |
|------|---------------------|
| "My dog won't sit still" | "There's never been a dog I haven't been able to photograph. We work at your dog's pace." |
| "My dog is reactive/anxious" | Share specific experience with reactive dogs. "I've worked with every breed, behaviour, and temperament." |
| "My dog won't cooperate" | "The best moments usually happen when dogs stop 'cooperating' and just do their thing." |
| "It's not the right time" | Gentle reframe about how there's never a perfect time, but now is always a good time |
| "It's too expensive" | Never discount. Instead, anchor to the emotional value: "These are the photos you'll reach for." |

## CTAs for Photography Clients

Always gentle. Always an open door, never a push.

**Good CTAs:**
- "If you've been thinking about doing a session, now's a good time."
- "I have a few spots open in the next couple of months."
- "You can check out [link] to see how it all works."
- "If you'd like to chat about what a session would look like for [dog's name], I'd love to hear from you."

**Bad CTAs (never use):**
- "Book now before spots fill up!"
- "Don't wait, you'll regret it!"
- "Limited availability, act fast!"
- "DM me NOW to secure your spot!"

## Email Frameworks

For client journey emails (enquiry responses, booking confirmations, pre-session, post-session), use the **3Ps Framework**:

1. **Purpose:** Why you're writing this email. Be clear.
2. **Personality:** Let Ina's warmth come through. This isn't a transaction, it's a relationship.
3. **Preview:** What happens next. Give them something to look forward to.

---

## Vocabulary Notes

- "Session" not "shoot" (shoot sounds aggressive)
- "Artwork" not "products" (artwork carries emotional weight)
- "Investment" is fine but don't overuse it. "Cost" or "pricing" are also okay, used naturally.
- "Your dog" or their dog's name when known, not "your pet" (too clinical)
- "Ordering appointment" or "reveal" not "sales session" (never let the sales framing show)
